                                Step 1 of 4

                                Welcome to Premade

                                Voice chat for League where champions sound louder the closer they are on the minimap. Setup takes a minute.

                                Step 2 of 4

                                Test your mic

                                Press Test mic, talk, and watch the bar. Use headphones so others don't hear themselves through your mic.

                                Step 3 of 4

                                Check your screen

                                Open a game of League (the Practice Tool works), then share the screen it's on. This checks that your minimap can be found.

                                Step 4 of 4

                                You're all set

                                Create a room on the Voice page and send your friends its code, or join theirs. You can change your name and mic later in Voice settings, at the bottom of that page.

                                Start End

                                Move Start and End, then save the cut as a new clip. Cuts land on the nearest second or two.

                                  What's new
                                  Friends join by typing this code on the site or in the Windows client, or by opening your invite link. Everyone in the same game should be in the same room.
                                  How far away you can hear other champions, in game units; 1,350 is about how far a champion sees. It starts at 1,200. Players are at full volume up to two thirds of it, then fade to silent at the edge. It only changes what you hear.
                                  Where everyone is in League, as the desktop app sees it: in the client, in a lobby, finding a match, in champ select, loading or in game. Players on the website show as not known, or in game once their minimap is tracked. Before a game everyone hears everyone; once it starts, you only hear players close to you on the map.
                                  Sharing your screen lets the page read your minimap and follow your champion's icon, so your camera can roam. It learns which icon is yours from your camera: early in a game, hold Space for a couple of seconds while you walk, or play with the camera locked. If the gold circle isn't on you, press Not my champion. In the Windows client it knows your champion from League itself.
                                  Only the host can lock the room: whoever made it, or once they leave, the player listed first (their card says host). Locking gives the room a password: friends already in stay in, and anyone joining with just the code has to type it. The invite link includes it, and it stays locked until everyone leaves. The host can also remove someone, which changes the password so they can't come back.
                                  The host picks the mode for everyone in the room. Classic: proximity chat, nothing else. Legends: your voice changes with the game: deep and booming with Baron, fiery with Elder, your team's dragon soul, bigger on a killing spree, a cocky echo after a steal, a stadium echo on an Ace, and cheering or gloomy after the game. Immersive: hollow and spooky while dead, watery in the river, a whoosh when you're really fast. Gibberish: the other team comes out as scrambled nonsense. Voice swap: every voice comes from someone else's spot on the map, reshuffled now and then, with a warning first and a reveal after. Chaos: Legends, Immersive and Gibberish at once. Everything but the river voice and Voice swap needs League's own data, so the desktop app.
                                  Clearer, fuller sound for a good mic with headphones. It turns off the browser's echo cancelling, noise cutting and volume levelling, and sends your voice at 96 kbps instead of about 32, which uses a bit more internet. Without headphones, others may hear themselves echo.
                                  An AI filter (RNNoise) that takes keyboard clicks, fans and background noise out of your mic while keeping your voice. Turn it off if your voice sounds robotic.
                                  Others only hear you while your mic is louder than this. Talk normally and watch the bar, then raise the gate until typing and background noise stay below it but your voice doesn't.
                                  Where everyone's voices come out. Using Wave Link? Pick its Voice Chat channel here, the same way you would in Discord.
                                  Shortcuts need Ctrl, Alt or Shift with a letter, number or F key, so they don't clash with League. The Windows client catches them while League is in front; a browser only while this page is.
                                  With a push-to-talk key, others only hear you while you hold it. Mouse side buttons work too. In the Windows client it works while League is in front; in a browser only while this page is.
                                  The screen the client shares to read your minimap. Pick the one League is on; the overlay shows up on that screen too.
                                  A small see-through bubble on top of League with what you tick here. League must be in Borderless mode (Settings, Video, Window Mode) for anything to show on top of it. Press Move it to drag it anywhere on the League screen. On the loading screen it shows all ten players, like Porofessor: your team on the left at the top, theirs below, with their rank, their record on their champion and tags, and the tips for your lane on the right (app 1.10). In a game, the overlay shortcut (Keyboard shortcuts) shows everyone's rank again; press it again to hide the overlay, and once more to bring it back. The CS counter is your CS a minute against your usual on that champion; League only updates it every 10 CS.
                                  While a game is on, this window shrinks to a strip with your mic and who's talking, and grows back when the game ends.
                                  When a game loads, the client joins a room made for that game, so everyone in it with the client hears each other without a code. It never moves you out of a room you joined yourself.
                                  Each game is saved in this app when it ends: who was in voice, how long everyone talked, and the talk map. Only on this computer.
                                  Best picks for you weighs each champion you may pick for your role: how it does there in high elo, how it does in high elo against their champion in your lane (their pick that plays there most), your own games and mastery on it, and what your team still lacks (magic or physical damage, a tank). Click one to hover it, and again on your turn to lock it in. On your ban turn, Bans for you lists the champions you lose to most, then the strongest in your role in high elo, leaving out the ones you play. Your team's ranks and last 20 games come from League's own client, with tags: a win streak, a first game on a champion, players you've met before, premades, someone who rarely plays their role, a possible smurf. Riot hides other players' names in Ranked Solo/Duo champ select, and so does this page. Once you lock in, the app makes a rune page for your champion and role (named LPC and the champion: the most popular one in high elo, Riot's recommended one, or your own usual one, Pregame settings), sets the summoner spells and puts the high elo build and your usual build in League's shop. Tips for your lane show up once your champion and the one in your lane are known: how that matchup goes in high elo, the runes, spells and early items high elo players take in it, your own games against it, its range and damage, Riot's own tips for both champions and your matchup note. They show again on the loading screen, but not in the game itself, as Riot's rules ask. High elo is Korea's Challenger and Grandmaster ranked games our server counts from Riot.
                                  The strongest champions in each role in high elo: Korea's Challenger and Grandmaster ranked games our server counts from Riot, this patch (with the last one added while this one is new). The tier comes from a champion's win rate, pulled toward 50% while it has few games so a lucky streak doesn't top the list: S, then A, B, C and D. Picked is how many of the games had it in that role. A champion shows once it has 10 games.
                                  The draft: your team on top and theirs below, each champion in its role (theirs are guessed from where each plays in high elo) with its score in this draft, and each team's total. Pick a role between them to rank every champion for it: Meta by its high elo win rate in that role, Matchups by how it does in high elo against their champion in that lane and, as our server counts them, with your team's champions and against the rest of theirs, and Balanced by both. A score is win rate points above or below 50%, pulled toward what the champions' own win rates would give while there are few games. Click a champion to hover it (or ban it on your ban turn), and again on your turn to lock it in. Right-click one to add it to your favorites, which come first. Type anywhere on this page to find a champion, and Enter hovers the first one. High elo is Korea's Challenger and Grandmaster ranked games our server counts from Riot.
                                  From the loading screen on: everyone's rank and last 20 games, when the next Dragon (or Elder) and Baron come, inhibitors coming back, and the gold difference from everyone's items, as Tab shows them. Riot doesn't allow timers for enemy abilities or summoner spells, so there are none.
                                  Start the queue, accept matches, pick or ban champions and answer trades from your phone, on any network: scan the code with your phone's camera. Your phone buzzes and rings when a match is found or it's your turn, while the page is open. Add it to your home screen to use it like an app. It works while this app is open on your PC. Anyone with the link can use it, so keep it to yourself; New code makes a fresh one.
                                  The desktop app talks to League's own client on this PC, through the local connection League offers apps. It never reads the game's memory. Accept matches for me waits a moment, then accepts every match found while the app runs. Hover for me hovers the first champion on your list that's free and fits your role as soon as champ select starts, and your ban on your ban turn; you still lock in yourself.
                                  From your games League's client has in detail (the app reads them one at a time while League is open, the newest 150): the champions you met in your lane at least three times and lost to more often than not, and your teammates' champions you won with most. Only on this computer.
                                  Games for the whole room, started by the host. Balance teams: two even teams for a custom game, from everyone's Solo/Duo rank (shared by the desktop app with League; anyone whose rank isn't known counts as the room's average). Role roulette: a random role each, different within each team; in the desktop app, one click puts yours first in League's lobby. Queue quiz: ten quick questions from Riot's champion and item lists, where fast right answers score most; it ends when the host's League finds a match. Mock draft: practice a Clash-style draft, bans and picks, 30 seconds a turn; anyone on the side whose turn it is can choose. Room awards come after each game with friends in voice, from talk time and League's scoreboard.
                                  Follow friends by their Riot ID to see their rank and LP go up and down, with a popup when it changes. The app looks each one up about every 10 minutes while League is open, through League's own client. Only on this computer.
                                  During a game the app keeps the last part of the screen League is on in memory, with what you hear and your mic. The clip key (Settings, Keyboard shortcuts), a double kill or a big moment saves it as a video in Videos\Premade; nothing is saved otherwise. Here you can play, cut, rename and star your clips, or drag one straight into Discord. Starred clips are never binned when the folder gets too big. Recording takes some of your computer's power: 720p or turning clips off helps if League stutters.
                                  Faster sends your friends' voices to you in smaller pieces, so they arrive a little sooner; it uses a bit more internet. Less data sends fewer, bigger pieces at a lower quality, both ways, for anyone on slow internet. Changing it reconnects voice for a moment.
                                  Saves what League tells the app during each game (its game data and what champ select and match history said) in Documents\Premade\Test recordings, with every name and account id swapped for Player 1, Player 2 and so on. No voice and no video. Send one to whoever builds the app so new features get tried on a real game. The newest 20 are kept.
                                  Back up saves your settings, matchup notes, LP history, game history, reports and clip favorites in one file. Restore loads such a file, on this PC or another, and replaces what's here.
                                  Your LP after each ranked game you play with the app open, kept on this computer: today's wins, losses and LP, and a chart of your rank.
                                  Your last 100 games from Riot's match history, and a report for each game: a score out of 100 for every player from KDA, kill share, damage dealt or taken, CS or vision, and gold. MVP is the best on the winning team, ACE the best on the losing team. A report also has the game's replay, your laning at 10 and 15 minutes, and when everyone bought each item.
