                                                          What's new

                                                          Friends join by typing this code on the site or in the Windows client, or by opening your invite link. Everyone in the same game should be in the same room.
                                                          How far away you can hear other champions, in game units; 1,350 is about how far a champion sees. It starts at 1,200. Voices fade to silent at the edge, the way Fade says. It only changes what you hear.
                                                          How voices fade as their champions move away; all of them reach silence at the edge of your hearing range. Normal: full volume up to two thirds of the range, then down to silent. Gentle: quieter little by little from close by. Realistic: like real sound, half as loud each time the distance doubles. Sharp: full volume until just before the edge. The dashed ring on the map shows where the fade starts.
                                                          Where everyone is in League, as the desktop app sees it: in the client, in a lobby, finding a match, in champ select, loading or in game. Players on the website show as not known, or in game once their minimap is tracked. Before a game everyone hears everyone; once it starts, you only hear players close to you on the map.
                                                          Sharing your screen lets the page read your minimap and follow your champion's icon, so your camera can roam. It learns which icon is yours from your camera: early in a game, hold Space for a couple of seconds while you walk, or play with the camera locked. If the gold circle isn't on you, press Not my champion. In the Windows client it knows your champion from League itself.
                                                          Only the host can lock the room: whoever made it, or once they leave, the player listed first (their card says host). Locking gives the room a password: friends already in stay in, and anyone joining with just the code has to type it. The invite link includes it, and it stays locked until everyone leaves. The host can also remove someone, which changes the password so they can't come back. Our server checks the password too, so nobody without it gets through to anyone in the room. The host can also mute a noisy mic for everyone (Mute for all on a card), and that player sees why.
                                                          The host picks the mode for everyone in the room. Classic: proximity chat, nothing else. Legends: your voice changes with the game: deep and booming with Baron, fiery with Elder, your team's dragon soul, a big metal shell with Herald, buzzing with Voidgrubs, crumbling stone when your team takes a tower, a super minion's when it takes an inhibitor, bigger on a killing spree, a cocky echo after a steal, a stadium echo on an Ace, and cheering or gloomy after the game. Immersive: hollow and spooky while dead, watery in the river, a whoosh when you're really fast. Gibberish: the other team comes out as scrambled nonsense. Voice swap: every voice comes from someone else's spot on the map, reshuffled now and then, with a warning first and a reveal after. Showdown: when you and one enemy are alone near each other, everyone else fades, so you hear only each other. Chaos: Legends, Immersive and Gibberish at once. Everything but the river voice and Voice swap needs League's own data, so the desktop app. In Voice swap you can also guess who's behind each voice: right guesses score at each reveal.
                                                          Clearer, fuller sound for a good mic with headphones. It turns off the browser's echo cancelling, noise cutting and volume levelling, and sends your voice at 96 kbps instead of about 32, which uses a bit more internet. Without headphones, others may hear themselves echo.
                                                          An AI filter (RNNoise) that takes keyboard clicks, fans and background noise out of your mic while keeping your voice. Turn it off if your voice sounds robotic.
                                                          Others only hear you while your mic is louder than this. Talk normally and watch the bar, then raise the gate until typing and background noise stay below it but your voice doesn't. Or press Set the gate for me and stay quiet for 3 seconds: it listens to your room and puts the gate just above its noise.
                                                          Where everyone's voices come out. Using Wave Link? Pick its Voice Chat channel here, the same way you would in Discord.
                                                          When it's on, the app listens to your mic during games for “Premade, clip that” and your own phrase, and saves a clip as the clip key does, even while you're muted. The speech recognition (Vosk) runs on this computer: nothing you say is sent anywhere or kept. The first time it downloads its English speech model, about 40 MB, and during games it takes about 200 MB of memory. Your own phrase: press Record my phrase and say it, or type it, in English, with at least two words you wouldn't say by accident.
                                                          Shortcuts need Ctrl, Alt or Shift with a letter, number or F key, so they don't clash with League. The Windows client catches them while League is in front; a browser only while this page is.
                                                          With a push-to-talk key, others only hear you while you hold it. Mouse side buttons work too. In the Windows client it works while League is in front; in a browser only while this page is.
                                                          The screen the client shares to read your minimap. Pick the one League is on; the overlay shows up on that screen too.
                                                          A see-through layer on top of League with what you tick here. League must be in Borderless mode (Settings, Video, Window Mode) for anything to show on top of it. In Layout, drag a part anywhere on your screen, or drag the rest; Put it back returns the part you picked to where it started. Move it lets you drag the rest on the League screen itself. On the loading screen it shows all ten players, like Porofessor: your team on the left at the top, theirs below, with their rank, their record on their champion and tags, and the tips for your lane on the right (app 1.10). In a game, the overlay shortcut (Keyboard shortcuts) shows everyone's rank again; press it again to hide the overlay, and once more to bring it back. The CS counter is your CS a minute against your usual on that champion; League only updates it every 10 CS. My lane is your CS and the gold in your items against the player in your lane on the other team.
                                                          For new players, to make glancing at the minimap a habit: “Check your minimap” shows at the top of the screen for 2 seconds, every 10 seconds in your first game with it and a second longer apart each game after that. After 20 games it turns itself off. Summoner's Rift only. It goes by League's clock alone, never by what happens in the game.
                                                          For new players, to learn to watch for the other team’s jungler: when their jungler shows up on your minimap, “Jungler on topside” (or botside, or in mid) shows at the top of the screen for 3 seconds, and again each time they show up after 10 seconds out of sight. Text only, unless Read callouts out loud is on. It reads only your minimap from your screen, so it never knows more than you can see. Summoner's Rift only.
                                                          Each callout shows at the top of the screen for 3 seconds, the most urgent first. Their jungler heading your way: when your minimap shows their jungler coming toward you. Missing laners: when a player you saw in a lane for a few seconds has been out of sight for 10 seconds, like “Zed missing from mid”. Enemies in your jungle: when they come in, like a level 1 invade, and again after 20 seconds without any. Who’s off your minimap: everyone on their team you can’t see, how long since you last saw them and where they were heading; a dead one shows about when they’re back. In Summoner’s Rift games, Premade reads your minimap from the screen in Settings, Screen, by itself, and nothing leaves your computer unless you’re in a voice room. Read callouts out loud uses your computer’s own voice.
                                                          Enemy timers: during a game, say “Premade, mid flash”, “Premade, top ultimate” (top, jungle, mid, bot or support) or “Premade, their red” (or blue), or tap them on your phone remote, and the countdown shows with the objective timers on the overlay, the Game page and your phone, for everyone in your voice room in that game who has them on. Flash takes 5 minutes and an ultimate its cooldown at their level, less the haste in their items; a buff is back 5 minutes after it’s taken. Tap a running one on your phone to take it off. Premade listens for these phrases on your computer, as it does for clips by voice. Power spikes: “Zed is level 6” at levels 6, 11 and 16, and “Jinx finished Infinity Edge”. Coach: a tip now and then at the top of the screen: when you have the gold for the next item of your build (app 1.14), an objective in 60 seconds, 3 or more of them dead (app 1.14), or 2 levels down on your lane opponent. I’m new adds why, and what to do. Jungle clock: at 2:45, where their jungler likely is when their first clear ends, from their own last games and where your minimap first showed them. These show at the top of the screen (app 1.10), and Read callouts out loud reads them too.
                                                          Inhouse ladder: every custom game you play to the end with the desktop app counts, for everyone in it: a rating that goes up when you win and down when you lose, more against a stronger team. Everyone in the room shares the games they have, so the ladder is the same for all of you, and Balance teams uses it once two of the room are on it. 1v1 referee: in a custom game of one player a side, the first of the rules you tick to happen wins, called at the top of the screen and kept in everyone’s 1v1 record. Fearless series (Room games, the host starts it): in the custom games after it starts, nobody picks a champion already played in it; champ select shows the ones used up.
                                                          While a game is on, this window shrinks to a strip with your mic and who's talking, and grows back when the game ends.
                                                          When a game loads, the client joins a room made for that game, so everyone in it with the client hears each other without a code. It never moves you out of a room you joined yourself.
                                                          Each game is saved in this app when it ends: who was in voice, how long everyone talked, and the talk map. Only on this computer.
                                                          Best picks for you weighs each champion you may pick for your role: how it does there in high elo, how it does in high elo against their champion in your lane (their pick that plays there most), your own games and mastery on it, and what your team still lacks (magic or physical damage, a tank). Click one to hover it, and again on your turn to lock it in. On your ban turn, Bans for you lists the champions you lose to most, then the strongest in your role in high elo, leaving out the ones you play. Your team's ranks and last 20 games come from League's own client, with tags: a win streak, a first game on a champion, players you've met before, premades, someone who rarely plays their role, a possible smurf. Riot hides other players' names in Ranked Solo/Duo champ select, and so does this page. Once you lock in, the app makes a rune page for your champion and role (named LPC and the champion: the most popular one in high elo, Riot's recommended one, or your own usual one, Pregame settings), sets the summoner spells and puts the high elo build and your usual build in League's shop. When high elo players take another rune page against your laner, a button puts that one on. Tips for your lane show up once your champion and the one in your lane are known: how that matchup goes in high elo, the runes, spells and early items high elo players take in it, your own games against it, its range and damage, Riot's own tips for both champions and your matchup note. ADCs and supports also get tips against their other bot laner, from the high elo games with that champion on the other team. They show again on the loading screen, but not in the game itself, as Riot's rules ask. High elo is Korea's Challenger and Grandmaster ranked games our server counts from Riot.
                                                          The strongest champions in each role in high elo: the Challenger and Grandmaster ranked games in Korea, Europe West and North America our server counts from Riot, this patch (with the last one added while this one is new). Games from switches to Emerald and Diamond games in Europe West and North America, once our server has some; below Master the list starts on those when a role has enough of them. The tier comes from a champion's win rate, pulled toward 50% while it has few games so a lucky streak doesn't top the list: S, then A, B, C and D. Picked is how many of the games had it in that role. A champion shows once it has 10 games. Click one to open its page in that role. An arrow shows a champion rising (▲) or falling (▼) since last patch: its win rate moved more than chance would, with 30 games or more in each patch. Banned is how many of the games had it banned, once our server counts bans.
                                                          Pick a champion and the one it plays against to see how that matchup goes in high elo: the record, gold and CS at 10 minutes, the runes, spells and items players take in it, the rune page and build most of those games had, your own games and Riot's tips for both. The role is the one the first champion plays most in high elo, unless you pick one. Like the other tips, it hides during a game, as Riot's rules ask. High elo is Korea's Challenger and Grandmaster ranked games our server counts from Riot.
                                                          The draft: your team on top and theirs below, each champion in its role (theirs are guessed from where each plays in high elo) with its score in this draft, and each team's total. Pick a role between them to rank every champion for it: Meta by its high elo win rate in that role, Matchups by how it does in high elo against their champion in that lane, with your team's champions and against the rest of theirs, and Balanced by both. A score is win rate points above or below 50%, pulled toward what the champions' own win rates would give while there are few games. Click a champion to hover it (or ban it on your ban turn), and again on your turn to lock it in. While their laner isn't picked yet, Matchups and Balanced also count how often the champions they may still pick beat it in that lane, so safe blind picks come first. Right-click one to add it to your favorites, which come first. Type anywhere on this page to find a champion, and Enter hovers the first one. High elo is Korea's Challenger and Grandmaster ranked games our server counts from Riot.
                                                          A 10-minute last-hitting test: start a Practice Tool game (Pregame, Practice Tool), play one lane and last-hit minions until 10:00 on the game clock. Premade saves your CS at 10:00, so you can see yourself improve, and the overlay counts down. League's game data only counts CS in tens, so 80+ means 80 to 89: type the exact number League showed at 10:00 to save it. Skipping time or extra minions in Practice Tool makes the score meaningless.
                                                          From the loading screen on: everyone's rank and last 20 games, when the next Dragon (or Elder) and Baron come, inhibitors coming back, and the gold difference from everyone's items, as Tab shows them. Riot doesn't allow timers for enemy abilities or summoner spells, so there are none.
                                                          Once a game is over: how you played against your usual games on that champion (from your last 100), high elo players on it in your role (Korea's Challenger and Grandmaster games our server counts) and your lane opponent, with what stood out; your gold and CS against your lane opponent minute by minute; where you got kills and died; the objectives each team took; then everyone's score, your laning and when everyone bought each item. From Riot's match history and the game's timeline. Riot doesn't allow advice during a game, so it comes after.
                                                          Start the queue, accept matches, pick or ban champions, answer trades and write in the room chat from your phone, on any network: scan the code with your phone's camera. Your phone buzzes and rings when a match is found or it's your turn, while the page is open. Tick “Tell me when a match is found” on the phone and it gets a notification even with its screen off or the page closed (on an iPhone, open the remote from its home screen icon first). Add it to your home screen to use it like an app. It works while this app is open on your PC. Anyone with the link can use it, so keep it to yourself; New code makes a fresh one.
                                                          The desktop app talks to League's own client on this PC, through the local connection League offers apps. It never reads the game's memory. Accept matches for me waits a moment, then accepts every match found while the app runs. Hover for me hovers the first champion on your list that's free and fits your role as soon as champ select starts, and your ban on your ban turn; you still lock in yourself.
                                                          From your games League's client has in detail (the app reads them one at a time while League is open, the newest 150): the champions you met in your lane at least three times and lost to more often than not, and your teammates' champions you won with most. Only on this computer.
                                                          Games for the whole room, started by the host. Balance teams: two even teams for a custom game, from the inhouse ladder once two of the room are on it (Customs, below), else from everyone's Solo/Duo rank (shared by the desktop app with League; anyone whose rating or rank isn't known counts as the room's average). Role roulette: a random role each, different within each team; in the desktop app, one click puts yours first in League's lobby. Queue quiz: ten quick questions from Riot's champion and item lists (titles, pictures, abilities, splash art close-ups, stories with the name blanked out, items and their costs), where fast right answers score most; it ends when the host's League finds a match. MVP vote: after a game with friends in voice everyone votes for the MVP, and the winner wears a crown on their card next game; the host can also start one. Mock draft: practice a Clash-style draft, bans and picks, 30 seconds a turn; anyone on the side whose turn it is can choose. Room awards come after each game with friends in voice, from talk time and League's scoreboard. Fearless series: custom games where nobody picks a champion already played in the series; champ select shows the ones used up (Customs, below).
                                                          Tags come from the last 20 games, mastery, level and Riot's ranked flags. Green and red ones are good to know, gold ones are just for fun. A few (kill participation, solo kills, steals, pings) are only in Riot's own match data, so they don't show for games from League's client. Past ranks: League's client tells the desktop app where last season ended and its peak, and this season's peak, for players on your server, and the app keeps yours for when League is closed. Riot doesn't give older seasons, so our server keeps every rank it gets from Riot, season by season, and shows those seasons once they're over. Nothing from other sites.
                                                          Follow friends by their Riot ID to see their rank and LP go up and down, with a popup when it changes. Under them, their latest games, rank-ups and pentakills, with a popup for a rank-up or a pentakill. The app looks each one up about every 10 minutes while League is open, through League's own client. Only on this computer.
                                                          A team code is League’s own way to pass a TFT comp around. Copy team code, then in League open the Team Planner (the TFT tab, beside Play, or top right in a game) and press Paste. Codes from friends, streamers and sites open here too. A code holds up to 10 champions, not their items, and only works in the set it was made for. The champions and their numbers come from League’s game data, through CommunityDragon.
                                                          During a game the app keeps the last part of the screen League is on in memory, with what you hear and your mic. The clip key, saying “Premade, clip that” (Clip settings, below), a double kill or a big moment saves it as a video in Videos\Premade; nothing is saved otherwise. Here you can play, cut, rename and star your clips, or drag one straight into Discord. Starred clips are never binned when the folder gets too big. Recording takes some of your computer's power: 720p or turning clips off helps if League stutters.
                                                          Faster sends your friends' voices to you in smaller pieces, so they arrive a little sooner; it uses a bit more internet. Less data sends fewer, bigger pieces at a lower quality, both ways, for anyone on slow internet. Changing it reconnects voice for a moment. Whatever you pick, when a friend starts losing some of your voice on the way, it goes to them in lower quality by itself until that stops, without reconnecting.
                                                          Saves what League tells the app during each game (its game data and what champ select and match history said) in Documents\Premade\Test recordings, with every name and account id swapped for Player 1, Player 2 and so on. No voice and no video. Send one to whoever builds the app so new features get tried on a real game. The newest 20 are kept.
                                                          Back up saves your settings, matchup notes, LP history, game history, reports and clip favorites in one file. Restore loads such a file, on this PC or another, and replaces what's here.
                                                          Your LP after each ranked game you play with the app open, kept on this computer: today's wins, losses and LP, and a chart of your rank. Pick a goal to see how many wins it takes at the LP you usually win and lose, and how many games at your win rate. Each League account you log in with keeps its own LP and profile; switch between them in Account, here or on Profile.
                                                          From your last 100 games on Summoner's Rift, each champion in the role you play it most. Keep: you win with it. Practice: strong in that role in high elo (S or A tier), but you lose more than you win with it or haven't played it much. Drop: you lose more than you win with it, and it isn't strong in high elo either. Win rates are pulled toward 50% while games are few, so one lucky game doesn't count for much. High elo is Korea's Challenger and Grandmaster ranked games our server counts from Riot.
                                                          Our server asks Riot which 8 champions each Challenger and Grandmaster player in Korea, Europe West and North America has the most mastery points on. For your three most played champions, these are the champions the most of those players also have, for how common each is overall, leaving out the ones you already play most. 40% of Ahri players means 40% of the players with Ahri among their 8 have that champion too.
                                                          From your last 100 games: your win rate in the game right after a win, a loss, or 2 or 3 losses in a row on the same day, and in your first to fifth game of the day. A day runs from 6:00 to 6:00, so a late night counts with its evening. Stop after shows when your win rate after losses in a row drops 5 points or more below your usual, with 5 games or more to go by.
